Cold. Wet. Intercepted.

The Weather Won

There are weeks when football is decided by preparation, execution and talent. And then there are weeks when it's cold, raining, nobody can feel their fingers, and half the league forgets how to throw a football.

Cold and rain affected games throughout the day. Balls were slippery. Receivers struggled. Quarterbacks struggled more.

For most of this season we've spent considerable editorial resources documenting Dylan Horsley's relationship with the interception. After watching Levi Knudson throw four of them in the rain, we're beginning to wonder if we've been too hard on Dylan.

Maybe Dylan hasn't been throwing like this all year. Maybe it's just been raining wherever Dylan is standing.

That's the only apology he's getting.

Team 8 damage report

Apparently Joseph Was Holding This Whole Thing Together

Joseph Timpson missed Week 6. His team responded by losing 34–12, with Levi Knudson going 14-of-34 for 177 yards, two touchdowns and four interceptions.

There may, however, be an explanation for at least one of those interceptions. Nathan Knudson has quietly been building an impressive defensive stat line, and sources inside Flag Football Weekly are investigating whether Levi was simply trying to help pad his brother's numbers.

Unfortunately, the throw was so bad Levi couldn't even get it to the right defender.

Brotherly generosity only works if you can hit your target.

Jesse Timpson was also spotted yelling at Jay Timpson during the game. Jay finished without a reception, which would seem to provide an obvious explanation. But those close to the situation weren't entirely convinced the discussion was football related.

Flag Football Weekly will respect the Timpson family's privacy while simultaneously continuing to speculate irresponsibly.

Hot streak watch

Gilbert Cawley Is Officially on a Heater

Gilbert's team moved to 4–2 with a convincing 34–12 victory, and Gilbert delivered one of his best performances of the season despite the conditions:

21-of-32   |   264 yards   |   5 TD   |   0 INT

Nathan Knudson caught six passes for 99 yards and two touchdowns. Adam Naylor added 78 yards and two scores. Will Naylor found the end zone as well.

At some point the investigation into Gilbert's quarterback success may need to shift toward roster construction. It turns out quarterbacking becomes slightly easier when you're surrounded by good receivers and Adam Naylor is wandering around the field looking like he should be checking defensive ends for their credentials.

Still, five touchdowns and zero interceptions is five touchdowns and zero interceptions.

The Gilbert Cawley Longevity Watch has officially expanded into the Gilbert Cawley Hot Streak Watch. We don't know how long this lasts. Based on Gilbert's age, possibly several decades.

Coaching carousel

Aaron Timpson: Better at Quarterback Than Offensive Coordinator

Aaron Timpson still isn't playing. This has not prevented Aaron Timpson from participating.

This week Aaron could be found in the backfield calling plays. Avery Hammon threw for 268 yards and four touchdowns. He also threw four interceptions. William Timpson's team walked away with the 34–25 victory.

There appears to be some information getting lost between Aaron's brain and Avery's arm.

Maybe Aaron's most effective play call all along has simply been: “Give me the ball.”

Aaron later appeared as a referee, where he apparently unveiled another innovative strategy: it's considerably easier not to call a quarterback getting tackled if you simply don't watch the quarterback get tackled.

EDITOR'S NOTE: The league is genuinely grateful for everyone who volunteers to referee these games. It's a thankless job, we couldn't play without people willing to do it, and their time and effort are sincerely appreciated. All officiating comments in Flag Football Weekly are made entirely in good fun.

That said, Aaron, please watch the quarterback.
Curt White 20, Lonnie Timpson 6

The Offensive Recession

Curt White and Lonnie Timpson combined for a breathtaking 26 points. Total.

Twenty points wasn't exactly an offensive explosion. It just looked like one compared to six.

The weather certainly didn't help, but Curt did exactly what he needed to do: score enough, hold Lonnie to one touchdown and get somewhere warm.

Sometimes it's cold and raining and 20 points makes you look like the Greatest Show on Turf.

Weatherproof department

Apparently Some People Can Play in the Rain

Gideon Hammon was the only receiver in Week 6 to break 100 receiving yards, finishing with 8 catches for 109 yards and a touchdown.

Daniel Dockstader also played extremely well despite the conditions, going 28-of-38 for 290 yards and five touchdowns in Donald Timpson's 41–19 win.

EDITOR'S NOTE: An anonymous source — who sounded suspiciously salty after his own team threw four interceptions — disputes our characterization of the conditions and claims the weather was “better during that game.”

Flag Football Weekly has been unable to independently verify whether the clouds specifically favored Gideon and Daniel's time slot. Our meteorological department consists primarily of looking outside.

One last thing

Final Thought

Week 6 may ultimately be remembered as the week the weather became the league's ninth defense.

Joseph disappeared and his team unraveled. Jesse yelled at Jay about something that may or may not have had anything to do with football. Aaron called plays, lost, then discovered an innovative approach to quarterback-contact officiating.

Lonnie scored six. Gideon somehow topped 100. Gilbert threw five touchdowns without an interception. Levi may have tried to pad his brother's defensive statistics and couldn't even get the ball to the right defender.

Cold. Wet. Chaotic. And somehow Gilbert Cawley was completely fine.
