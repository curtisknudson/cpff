Saturday's damage report

The Doubleheader Changed Everything

Three weeks into the season, Aaron Timpson and William Timpson were both 3-0. Forty-eight football hours later, neither was undefeated.

The biggest beneficiary was Cole White's Team 4, which demolished Dylan Horsley's team 47-6, then handed Aaron his second consecutive loss, 33-21. Team 4 scored 80 points while allowing just 27 and now sits atop the standings at 4-1 with a league-best +89 point differential.

William also emerged 4-1, although his route was considerably less peaceful. Team 3 lost a 53-44 shootout to Joseph's team before escaping Alonso 44-39 in Week 5.

Meanwhile, Joseph's Team 8 quietly had the weekend everyone else should be afraid of: 103 points in two games.

Main Event

The UNC Bowl

The schedule finally gave us the matchup everyone had been waiting for: Aaron Timpson vs. Gilbert Cawley. The two oldest quarterbacks in the league. A combined age that the Newsletter has been advised by counsel not to publish.

This was the UNC Bowl.

There was, however, a complication. Shortly before kickoff, Aaron was reportedly seen taking a hit to the ribs. When the game began, it was Avery Hammon - not Aaron - handling the primary quarterback duties for Team 2.

Avery finished 19-of-32 for 223 yards, four touchdowns and two interceptions. Gilbert went 21-of-34 for 268 yards, four touchdowns and one interception and walked away with the 34-25 victory.

So Gilbert won the inaugural UNC Bowl. But this newspaper is not yet prepared to close the investigation. Did Gilbert finally defeat his fellow elder statesman fair and square? Or did Aaron's rib cage alter the course of league history?

We report. You decide.

Father Time Finally Gets Home

Through three weeks, our investigation had found zero recorded sacks against Gilbert's team. Then came the UNC Bowl: four sacks, all recorded by Aaron Zitting, who now leads the league with six.

Gilbert also attempted four rushes for -14 yards. After three weeks of trying, Father Time finally got into the backfield. Apparently he was wearing Aaron Zitting's jersey.

And Gilbert still won.

Anonymous sources

Who Was "Sub Joseph"?

An anonymous source alleges that the quarterback previously identified only as "Sub Joseph" was Gilbert Cawley.

The Week 2 game sheet gives us a much cleaner fingerprint. In Week 2 only, Sub Joseph went 22-of-32 (68.8%) for 320 yards, six touchdowns, zero interceptions and a 140.6 passer rating. Those are the only Sub Joseph statistics this newspaper is attributing to Gilbert.

Week 1 also contains a separate Sub Joseph line - 22-of-40 for 312 yards, three touchdowns and one interception - but our source has not identified that Week 1 substitute as Gilbert, so those numbers stay out of his file.

If the source is correct and Gilbert was under center for Joseph in Week 2, perhaps the obvious conclusion isn't simply that Gilbert played great.

Maybe Joseph's receivers are really, really good.

Corrections Department

Caleb Barlow: We Misplaced 13 Inches

Last issue, Flag Football Weekly reported that Caleb Barlow stood 6'2" with a 24-inch vertical.

We have since learned that Caleb is actually 6'1" with a 37-inch vertical.

So we overestimated his height by one inch while underestimating his vertical by thirteen inches. This is among the more impressive measurement errors in the history of sports journalism.

Fortunately, his production has started cooperating. Caleb followed his Week 3 breakout with four catches for 64 yards and a touchdown in Week 4, then added six catches for 94 yards and three touchdowns in Week 5.

Through five weeks: 18 catches, 317 yards and five touchdowns.

Unfortunately, Team 6 is now 0-5. Caleb can apparently jump 37 inches. The standings remain just out of reach.

Injury desk

The Medical Report

Aaron Timpson - Ribs

Aaron was seen taking a shot to the ribs shortly before the UNC Bowl. He subsequently did not serve as Team 2's primary passer against Gilbert.

We have no medical evidence establishing that the two events are related. Naturally, we have placed them directly beside each other and allowed readers to draw conclusions.

STATUS: MONITORING

Daniel Williams - Back

First-round pick Daniel Williams entered the doubleheader questionable with a lingering back issue. The obvious question: did it slow him down?

Daniel caught 10 passes for 125 yards and three touchdowns in the UNC Bowl, then added another five catches for 54 yards in Week 5. Doubleheader total: 15 catches, 179 yards, three touchdowns.

If that's Daniel Williams playing hurt, opposing defenses may want to monitor his recovery more closely than he does.

Bruce Hammon - Shoulder

Bruce Hammon reportedly suffered a dislocated shoulder, and the league is currently awaiting an injury update.

Before exiting, however, Bruce left us with one final contribution: Lonnie ended his game by throwing a touchdown pass to Bruce as Bruce breezed past Gideon Hammon.

Jesse Timpson - Groin

Jesse Timpson played through an injured groin for both games of the doubleheader and is currently questionable for next week.

Apparently “injured” is a relative term. Jesse still managed seven catches for 110 yards and two touchdowns in Week 5 alone.

If that was Jesse at less than 100%, the Medical Report would prefer not to know what healthy looks like.

STATUS: QUESTIONABLE

Gideon Hammon - Ankles

The Medical Report has reached out to Gideon for an update on the current condition of his ankles.

As of publication, he has not responded.

We will respect his privacy during what we can only assume is a difficult recovery.

Player Highlight

Nathan Knudson: Week In, Week Out

Nathan Knudson has already received attention in this publication for having perhaps the strongest hands in the league. Five weeks in, the numbers aren't doing much to hurt that reputation:

40 receptions. 52 targets. 490 yards. Four touchdowns. Zero drops.

But the Newsletter would like to highlight something else: he keeps playing.

Nathan is the younger brother of the late Derrick Knudson, whose playing career developed a reputation for being remarkably injury-prone. Nathan has apparently taken a different approach.

Against Aaron's team: 12 catches, 138 yards, one touchdown. Against Donald's: 9 catches, 166 yards.

That's 21 receptions for 304 yards in one doubleheader, without a recorded drop.

No medical tent. No weekly questionable designation. Just another week of Nathan Knudson catching footballs.

Somewhere, Derrick has to be wondering where this version of the family durability was when he needed it.

The William Timpson Experience

William Timpson leads the league in passing yards (1,722) and passing touchdowns (29). Those are very good numbers. We now need to discuss the other number.

Through the first three weeks, William had thrown 17 touchdowns and just one interception. During the doubleheader: 12 touchdowns and seven interceptions.

Week 4 brought 278 yards, five touchdowns and three interceptions in the loss to Joseph. Week 5 brought an absurd 494 yards, seven touchdowns and four interceptions in a 44-39 victory over Alonso.

William's season remains spectacular. It has simply become considerably more interactive for the defense.

Dylan Interception Watch

At some point a recurring feature becomes a public service.

Dylan Horsley entered the doubleheader with 12 interceptions. He threw six more in Week 4. Then four more in Week 5.

Ten interceptions in one doubleheader. Twenty-two through five games.

Dylan has now thrown eight touchdowns and 22 interceptions this season.

The Newsletter initially considered Dylan's interception total a statistic. Then a trend. Then a recurring feature. We are currently researching whether it qualifies as its own department.

The Hands Investigation

We previously identified Nathan Knudson as possessing some of the league's strongest hands. Five weeks later: 40 catches. Zero drops. Case strengthened.

Jay Timpson's Hands Work Perfectly Fine

Last issue, Jay Timpson's drops were blamed on years of catching passes from left-handed quarterbacks and the difficult transition to the opposite spin.

We are pleased to announce a breakthrough. Jay now has four receiving drops. He also leads the entire league with six defensive interceptions.

Apparently Jay has no trouble catching footballs. He just prefers them thrown by the other team.

Of course, the Newsletter Investigative Unit has identified one potentially important variable: some of those interceptions may have come from left-handed quarterbacks. If so, Jay may not have solved his spin problem at all. He may simply have found a way to make sure the left-handed passes are coming from the other team.

The Sack Department

Aaron Zitting's UNC Bowl performance deserves more attention: four sacks in one game. He now has six on the season, twice as many as the next group of defenders at three.

That means Gilbert survived the league's most productive pass rusher having his best day of the season and still left with the win.

From the Editor's Desk

The Sample Sizes Are Getting Better

The doubleheader was supposed to give us clarity.

Instead, both undefeated teams lost, Cole took over first place, Joseph's offense scored 103 points, Gilbert finally got sacked, Aaron's ribs entered the news cycle, anonymous sources started identifying substitute players, the Corrections Department misplaced thirteen inches of Caleb Barlow's vertical, and the Medical Report is currently awaiting updates on one shoulder, one back, one rib cage and two allegedly compromised ankles.

We have five games of data now. That means the excuses are getting worse.

And the sample sizes are getting better.

Flag Football Weekly will continue to investigate.
