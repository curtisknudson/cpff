The Hands, the Size, the Speed

Team 5 (Gilbert Cawley) is becoming suspiciously well equipped. Nathan Knudson, who has some of the strongest hands in the league, has 19 catches on 24 targets for 186 yards and three touchdowns. George Timpson, one of Gilbert's fastest receivers, has 10 catches for 118 yards and three scores. Then there is Adam Naylor, one of the biggest players in the league, who just went for 116 yards and three touchdowns in Week 3.

Size, hands and speed. Gilbert has apparently decided the best way to extend a quarterback career is to outsource as much difficulty as possible.

Gilbert Cawley Longevity Watch

Team 5 (Gilbert Cawley) is 2-1, and Gilbert has 630 passing yards and nine touchdowns through three weeks. Week 3 brought 308 yards and four touchdowns despite three interceptions. The numbers remain annoyingly alive.

The Newsletter Investigative Unit originally went looking for sacks as evidence that the veteran quarterback might finally be slowing down. The problem: the recorded game sheets show zero sacks against Team 5 in all three games. Apparently even Father Time cannot get into the backfield.

There is, however, one mobility clue: Gilbert has exactly one recorded rush for -4 yards. We are not prepared to call that a decline. We are prepared to call it an extremely small sample size that is also very funny.

Official Longevity Status: Still upright. Still winning. Surrounded by size, hands and speed. Investigation remains open.

The Spin Zone — An Investigation Into Jay Timpson's Hands

Jay Timpson has 2 recorded drops through Week 3, but before criticizing him, context matters. Jay has spent the last three seasons catching passes from left-handed quarterbacks — including one season with Gilbert Cawley. Now he's being asked to adjust to the opposite spin of a right-handed ball. Scientists call this “rotation.” Jay calls it “a perfectly legitimate excuse.”

League sources confirm he may need another 3–5 seasons to fully adjust.

The Caleb Barlow Athleticism Watch

Caleb Barlow is 6'2", in excellent shape and has a 24-inch vertical. Through the first two games, all of that athletic equipment had produced only 2 catches for 21 yards. The stat sheet appeared unaware of the scouting report.

Then Week 3 happened: 6 catches, 138 yards and a touchdown. So the planned “where is the production?” investigation has been temporarily suspended due to the sudden appearance of production.

The defense is still fair game. Caleb plays safety for Team 6 (Dylan Horsley), which has allowed 15 offensive touchdowns — second-most in the league and only one behind the lead. At 6'2" with a 24-inch vertical, Caleb theoretically occupies a considerable amount of airspace. Opposing offenses have spent three weeks locating the rest.

The Tackle Leader Asterisk

Ean Dockstader leads the league with 19 tackles. Impressive — until you remember that tackles generally require the person you are defending to possess the football first.

We are not saying Team 4 (Cole White) cannot cover. We are merely noting that Ean has become extraordinarily experienced at ending plays after somebody else has already started them.

League investigation: Is “most tackles” a defensive award, or a highly detailed admission of guilt?
